Getting Started

Hackbeat is a music tracker with a twist: instead of the classic up-and-down pattern grid, time flows left to right, like a video editor timeline. Each horizontal lane is a channel. Notes live in cells along that lane.

The fastest way to hear something:

  1. Click a sample in the LIBRARY list (bottom-left). That loads it into the current instrument slot, shown in the INSTRUMENTS list above it.
  2. Click into the grid, then press a note key (Z, S, X, D, C… see Entering Notes). A note appears in the cell and the cursor moves forward.
  3. Press Space to hear the pattern play.

The Grid (Timeline)

Each row down the left is a channel — its own instrument, volume, mute/solo, and effects. Time runs across the row. Each cell holds three things, shown as note inst vol:

  • Note — which pitch plays, e.g. C-5. === means "note off" — it stops whatever is currently sounding on that channel.
  • Inst — which instrument slot to use. Leave it blank and the cell just uses whichever instrument is currently selected in the INSTRUMENTS list.
  • Vol — per-note volume, 0–64. Blank means full volume.

Splitting a cell: double-click any cell to split it into 2, 3, or 4 evenly-timed hits (double-click again to cycle, or use the Split buttons in the cell inspector on the right). Each hit holds its own note — perfect for 16ths, triplets, or rolls without changing the whole pattern's resolution.

Right-click a cell that has a note to open that cell's sample in the Sound Editor. From there, Apply to This Cell saves your edit as a new sample used by just that one cell, while Replace changes every cell that uses the sample.

Zoom (top toolbar, − / +) stretches or squeezes the whole timeline — handy when you're working on fine detail versus looking at the whole arrangement.

Turn the coloured waveform ribbons and the VU meters in each channel header on or off with the WAVE / VU buttons in the toolbar — purely visual, no effect on sound.

Entering Notes

Two rows of your keyboard act like piano keys, just like a classic tracker:

Lower rowZ S X D C V G B H N J M
Upper rowQ 2 W 3 E R 5 T 6 Y 7 U I 9 O 0 P

These map to a full chromatic scale starting at the Octave shown in the toolbar. Change the octave number to reach higher or lower notes.

` (backtick) enters a note-off (===) instead of a pitch — use it to cut a sustained sample short.

Notes only get written into the grid while REC (top toolbar) is lit up. Turn REC off and the same keys just preview sound without touching the pattern — handy for auditioning without risk of overwriting anything.

Channels

Each channel header (left side of its lane) has:

  • Name — click to rename.
  • M (Mute) — silences that channel.
  • S (Solo) — silences every other channel. Solo more than one channel and only those play.
  • STRIP — opens that channel's mixer strip and effects in the right-hand panel (see Mixing & Effects).

+ Channel / − Channel in the top toolbar add or remove lanes.

Mixing & Effects

Click STRIP on any channel to open its settings on the right:

  • Volume & Pan — basic level and left/right position.
  • 3-Band EQ — boost or cut low, mid, and high frequencies.
  • Insert effects — Bitcrusher, Distortion, and Chorus live entirely on that one channel.
  • Reverb Send / Delay Send — how much of this channel gets fed into the shared reverb and delay.

Why "send"? Reverb and delay are expensive to compute, so instead of giving every channel its own full reverb unit, Hackbeat runs one shared reverb and one shared delay that every channel can send into — exactly how a real mixing console works. That's why the actual reverb decay time and delay time/feedback aren't per-channel knobs — they live in SEND BUSES (GLOBAL) at the bottom of the panel, and changing them there affects every channel's send at once.

The master volume slider (top-right) sits after an automatic headroom stage and a limiter, so adding more channels won't suddenly cause harsh clipping — the mix quietly makes room for itself as tracks are added.

Instruments & the Library

There are two different sample lists in the left sidebar — it's worth knowing the difference:

  • INSTRUMENTS (top) — the samples actually loaded into this song, numbered 01, 02, 03… Pattern cells refer to these numbers.
  • LIBRARY (bottom) — every sample file sitting on disk, whether or not it's currently used in the song. Click one to load it into the next instrument slot.

Both lists have the same three buttons on each row:

  • ▶ — preview the sound.
  • ✎ — open it in the Sound Editor.
  • × — remove it. On the LIBRARY list this deletes the file from disk and will warn you if it's currently in use somewhere in the song. On the INSTRUMENTS list it just removes that slot from the song.

If a library file gets deleted while it's still loaded as an instrument, that instrument's row dims and is marked (missing) — its pattern cells will play silently until you load a replacement sample into that slot.

The Sound Editor

Double-click any sample (in either list) to open the editor. Drag on the waveform to select a region — most tools work on the selection, or the whole sample if nothing's selected.

  • Fade In / Fade Out — smooth ramps over the selection.
  • Normalize — raises the loudest point to just under full volume.
  • Trim — keeps only the selection, discards the rest.
  • Cut — removes the selection, keeps the rest.
  • Silence — mutes the selection without changing its length.
  • Reverse — plays the selection backwards.
  • −3dB / +3dB — quick gain nudges.
  • Undo — steps back through your last few edits.
  • FX dropdown — permanently bakes Distortion, Bitcrush, Lowpass/Highpass, Delay, or Reverb into the audio (unlike the channel sends, this actually changes the sample file).
  • ● Record — records from your microphone straight into the editor.

When you're happy, either:

  • Save as New — writes a new file, leaving the original untouched.
  • Replace — overwrites the original file everywhere it's used.

AI Sample Generation

The GENERATE panel (bottom-left, and also inside the Sound Editor) turns a text prompt into a new sample using MusicGen. Type a style or instrument description, set a duration, and hit Generate. First run downloads the model, so it'll be slow exactly once.

Style buttons: the Single / Ensemble / One Note buttons quietly append extra instructions to your prompt. Single pushes the model toward one unaccompanied instrument, Ensemble toward a full layered arrangement, and One Note toward a single sustained pitch — ideal when you want a clean playable instrument tone, since Hackbeat repitches from C-5. Click a button again to turn it off.

Hum or whistle a melody: hit ● Hum, record yourself humming or whistling, then type a style prompt (e.g. "warm analog synth lead") and Generate. Hackbeat extracts the melody's pitch contour and generates new audio that follows it in that style — this automatically switches to the melody-aware model.

You can also send audio already open in the Sound Editor as the melody, using its own Use audio as melody button — useful if you've recorded or trimmed a whistle first.

Saving, Loading & Export

  • Save — stores the whole song (patterns, instruments, mixer/FX settings, order) under the name in the text box next to it.
  • Load… dropdown — loads a previously saved song.
  • Your work also autosaves to this browser as you go, and restores automatically next time you open Hackbeat — separate from named Save/Load.
  • ORDER bar — the sequence of patterns that make up the whole song. + Add pattern appends the pattern you're currently viewing. If you never set an order, "play song" and "export" just play/render every pattern you've made, in order.
  • Export WAV — renders the whole song (following ORDER) to a downloadable 48kHz WAV file.

Troubleshooting

  • An instrument looks greyed out and says "(missing)". Its sample file was deleted from the LIBRARY. Load a different sample into that slot, or find the original file and re-import it.
  • Generate fails with an error about dependencies. The AI packages (torch/transformers) aren't installed in this copy's virtual environment — re-run the installer without the --no-ai flag.
  • Sound gets crackly or harsh with lots of channels. Check the master headroom is doing its job — try easing back individual channel volumes and the SEND BUSES send amounts rather than the master fader.
  • Recording or Humming doesn't prompt for the microphone. Browsers only allow mic access on localhost or secure (https) pages — make sure you're opening Hackbeat at http://localhost:8200.
